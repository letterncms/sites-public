It is nine in the evening, and the playroom on the children’s ward is finally quiet. For nurse Priya Nair and her three colleagues, the night has only just begun.

“Children are rarely ill on a schedule,” she says, smiling. Over the next ten hours, the team will check on eighteen children, settle a toddler with a fever, answer a worried mother’s questions at three in the morning, and welcome a teenager from the emergency department.

## Parents stay too

Every room on the ward has a fold-out bed for a parent. “Children heal better when someone they love is close,” says Priya. “And parents often notice something before we do.”

At seven, the day team arrives. Priya hands over each child, one by one, and goes home to sleep.
