When someone has a stroke, every minute without treatment costs nearly two million brain cells. Two years ago, our stroke team set itself a goal: treatment within thirty minutes of arriving at the hospital.

They now do better than that. Over the past year, patients who needed clot-dissolving medicine received it a median of 24 minutes after they came through the door, half the time it took before.

## What changed

The ambulance service now calls ahead, so a neurologist, a nurse and a radiographer meet the patient at the door. The patient goes straight to the CT scanner on the ambulance trolley, and the medicine is given in the scan room.

> “It sounds simple, but it took every team in the hospital working together to make it happen,” says Dr Folake Adeyemi, who leads the stroke unit.

## Know the signs

A drooping face, a weak arm or slurred speech: call 112 straight away.
