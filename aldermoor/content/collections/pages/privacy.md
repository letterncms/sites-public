The City of Aldermoor needs information about you to provide many of its services. This statement explains what we collect, why, how long we keep it, and what your rights are. It follows the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

## Who is responsible

The City of Aldermoor, City Hall, Market Square 1, 4811 Aldermoor, is responsible for your personal data. Our data protection officer checks that we handle it lawfully. You can reach them at privacy@aldermoor.example or by post at the address above, marked “Data protection officer”.

## What we collect on this website

- **When you send a form**, we keep the answers you give and the page you sent it from. We don’t store your IP address.
- **When you visit a page**, our server records the page and the time, to keep the website safe and working. We delete these records after 14 days.
- We don’t use analytics or advertising cookies, and we don’t show content from social media. See [cookies](/cookies).

## Why we use your data

We only use your data when the law allows it:

- to carry out our tasks as a public authority, such as handling your report or your application
- because the law requires it, such as registering a birth or collecting council tax
- with your permission, such as sending you updates about a report. You can withdraw your permission at any time

We never sell your data, and we only share it with organisations that work for us, under a contract that protects it, or when the law requires it.

## How long we keep it

We keep your data only as long as we need it for the service, or as long as the law tells us to. Questions sent through the contact form are deleted 2 years after we answer. Feedback on pages is kept without your name.

## Your rights

You have the right to see the data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to limit how we use it, to object, and to take it with you. To use your rights, email privacy@aldermoor.example. We reply within one month.

If you think we handle your data wrongly, tell our data protection officer. You can also complain to the national data protection authority.
