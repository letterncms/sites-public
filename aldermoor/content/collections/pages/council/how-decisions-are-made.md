Most decisions about the city’s services are made by the executive: the mayor and the four deputy mayors. Big decisions, such as the budget, the city’s plans and new rules, are made by the full council.

::: box {"props":{"tag":"ol"},"class":"steps"}
:::: box {"props":{"tag":"li"}}
**A plan is written.** Council officers write a proposal, often after asking residents in a [consultation](/have-your-say).
::::

:::: box {"props":{"tag":"li"}}
**A committee discusses it.** Councillors look at it closely in one of four committees, which meet in public.
::::

:::: box {"props":{"tag":"li"}}
**The council decides.** At a council meeting, councillors debate the plan and vote. A plan needs more than half of the votes.
::::

:::: box {"props":{"tag":"li"}}
**The decision is published.** Decisions are in the minutes on the [documents page](/documents) within a week.
::::

:::: box {"props":{"tag":"li"}}
**The executive carries it out.** Officers put the decision into practice and report back to the council.
::::
:::

## Committees

- **Planning**: building plans and changes to land use
- **Finance**: the budget, taxes and the annual accounts
- **Communities**: housing, care, schools and culture
- **City and environment**: transport, waste, parks and climate

## If you disagree with a decision

You can write to the council or to your councillor at any time. If a decision affects you personally, such as a refused permit, the letter about it tells you how to object and by when.
